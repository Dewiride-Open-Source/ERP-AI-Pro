# ADR-0056: The server's own files backed up nightly, sealed to an operator key, and the restore drill

Status: accepted
Date: 2026-10-31

## Context

- **What existed.** The database keeps 7 days of point-in-time restore on geo-redundant backup storage ([ADR-0050](0050-the-production-database-azure-sql-basic-with-microsoft-entra-only-users.md)), and the attachments are geo-redundant with 90 days of blob and container soft delete ([ADR-0051](0051-the-production-attachments-storage-geo-redundant-and-reachable-from-the-homes-address-alone.md)). Nothing kept a copy of what lives only on the server: the Data Protection key ring in the volume `erp-ai-pro-data-protection-keys` ([ADR-0032](0032-token-cache-in-sql-server-and-the-data-protection-key-ring.md)), the proxy's certificate authority and certificates in `erp-ai-pro-proxy-data` ([ADR-0048](0048-the-edge-proxy-caddy-on-the-servers-local-address.md)), and `infra/compose/.env` and `.env.previous`, which name the running release and the one before ([ADR-0053](0053-the-release-public-images-a-dependabot-gate-attestations-verified-on-the-server-and-the-deployment-scripts.md)). The client certificates live in `kv-erp-ai-pro-ops` and the images on ghcr.io, so neither needs a copy.
- **Facts that shaped the design** (official sources, read 2026-10-30 and 2026-10-31).
  - Azure Backup does not back up files of an on-premises Linux server: the MARS agent runs on Windows alone, and MABS protects Linux only as a Hyper-V or VMware guest (learn.microsoft.com, Azure Backup support matrix). The Microsoft-first answer is a job of our own writing to Blob Storage.
  - A container-level time-based retention policy keeps every blob write-once: blobs "can be created and read, but not modified or deleted" for the interval counted from each blob's creation, and afterwards deleted but never overwritten. An unlocked policy can be shortened or removed; a locked one only lengthened, at most five times, and an account cannot be deleted while a container under a locked policy exists. Lifecycle rules cannot delete blobs in an immutable container (learn.microsoft.com, immutable storage overview, container-level WORM policies, lifecycle management overview).
  - A locked policy accepts the extend action alone, so a deployment that sends the policy's create-or-update fails once it is locked (Azure CLI and storage resource provider reference).
  - `openssl cms -encrypt` seals a message for recipient certificates; AES-GCM is its only authenticated mode, RSA-OAEP needs `-recip` with `-keyopt`, and the default cipher is triple DES in OpenSSL 3.0 (docs.openssl.org). Ubuntu ships OpenSSL 3.0.2, 3.0.13 and 3.5.5 on 22.04, 24.04 and 26.04.
  - `az login --service-principal` takes a PEM holding both the private key and the certificate, and `--skip-subscription-discovery` signs in an identity that holds no subscription role (learn.microsoft.com, Azure CLI).
  - Microsoft's distroless debug image of Azure Linux adds BusyBox (with `tar`) to the distroless base, and the Azure CLI image installs the CLI on Azure Linux and sets no user (learn.microsoft.com, Azure Linux container images; github.com/Azure/azure-cli).
  - A database restore creates a new database and never overwrites one; a restored database can replace the original by renaming the two with `ALTER DATABASE … MODIFY NAME` (learn.microsoft.com, "Restore a database from a backup in Azure SQL Database"). Long-term retention is available for Basic, outlives the deletion of the database and of the server, and is billed by the storage it uses (₹5.4714 per GB-month on the Central India price list of 2026-10-30).
  - Azure refuses a long-term retention policy that names a `yearlyRetention`, even `PT0S`, without a `weekOfYear` of 1 to 52 (`LongTermRetentionMissingWeekOfYear`, seen on the first deployment of 2026-10-31), and reads an unset one back as `PT0S`.
- **Owner decisions of 2026-10-30.** "Sealed 90 days": nightly encrypted copies of the server's own files in a storage account of their own, write-once for 90 days, locked once the first restore rehearsal succeeds, sealed to a key only the operator opens, with no alert when a backup fails (the answer to [ADR-0055](0055-the-production-host-restarts-an-unhealthy-container-and-alerts-nobody.md)'s question applies). "Weekly + monthly copies": the database keeps each weekly backup for 5 weeks and the first of each month for 12 months; the attachments keep what protects them now. A full rehearsal twice a year and after any change to the backups, the ERP closed about 90 minutes, with targets of 4 hours to work again, at most 15 minutes of database work lost and no attached file lost.

## Decision

- **The database** gains `backupLongTermRetentionPolicies/default` in `sql-database.bicep`: `weeklyRetention` `P5W`, `monthlyRetention` `P12M` and `timeBasedImmutability` `Disabled`, naming no yearly retention. `verify.sh --database` reads the policy back and expects `PT0S` for the yearly copy.
- **The backup account.** `provision.sh` deploys `storage-account.bicep` a third time as `sterpaiprobackup` (`ERP_AZURE_STORAGE_BACKUP_NAME`): `Standard_GRS`, Shared Key off, the home's address alone (`defaultAction` `Deny`, `bypass` `None`, one IP rule that `home-address.sh` moves with the other two), 7 days of blob and container soft delete, versioning and the change feed off and the `CanNotDelete` lock. Its one private container `host-backups` carries a 90-day time-based retention policy without protected append writes, created unlocked. `provision.sh` reads the policy's state before every deployment and leaves a locked policy out of the template, so later deployments never send the create-or-update a locked policy refuses; locking it is the operator's deliberate step after the first rehearsal, with the policy's ETag ([Azure bootstrap](../operations/azure-bootstrap.md), section 5f). `verify.sh` checks the account like the production one, the policy (90 days, no protected append writes, no legal hold) and warns while it is unlocked.
- **The backup identity.** `entra.sh` converges a fifth registration, "ERP-AI-Pro Backup (production)", with no redirect URI and its certificate `Erp--Platform--Identity--BackupClientCertificate` in `kv-erp-ai-pro-ops` as its only credential. Its one role is Storage Blob Data Contributor on `host-backups`, and nobody else holds a role on the account or in it: the write-once policy, not the role, stops it overwriting an archive or deleting a young one.
- **The seal.** `entra.sh` creates `Erp--Platform--Backup--EncryptionCertificate` in `kv-erp-ai-pro-ops` from its own policy template: self-signed RSA 4096, exportable, `keyEncipherment` alone, 12 months, a new key per version, rotated yearly with `--rotate-encryption-certificate`. The server holds its public certificate alone (`secrets/erp-backup-recipient.crt`, placed by `bootstrap.sh --backup-recipient`, which refuses a private key and a key under 4096 bits), so it can seal an archive and never open one, an old one included. The private key leaves the vault only for a restore (`export-client-certificate.sh backup-recipient-key`), and `restore.sh` shreds the copy on the server.
- **The nightly job.** `erp-ai-pro-backup.timer` runs `erp-ai-pro-backup.service` at 02:00 India time (`Persistent=true`), as `erp-ai-pro` in the compose folder, with a private runtime folder under `/run` and `UMask=0077`, skipped until `.env`, the client certificate and the recipient certificate exist. `scripts/deploy/backup.sh`:
  - reads `BACKUP_BLOB_ENDPOINT`, `BACKUP_CONTAINER`, `BACKUP_AZURE_CLIENT_ID` and `AZURE_TENANT_ID` from `.env` as data, never sourced, refusing another form, a duplicate or a value of another shape;
  - pulls the two images of `infra/compose/compose.backup.yaml` before it takes the lock;
  - takes `/srv/erp-ai-pro/deploy.lock` with the same bounded wait as `deploy.sh` (`acquire_release_lock`), reads each volume through a BusyBox reader that runs as the volume's owner (1654 or 10001), with the volume mounted read-only, no network, a read-only root, every capability dropped and `no-new-privileges`, copies `.env` and `.env.previous`, and lets the lock go;
  - writes a manifest (format `ERP-AI-Pro server backup 1`: the time, the host, the releases both files name, the proxy root's SHA-256 fingerprint, each key of the ring with its expiry and the Key Vault key version that wraps it, and each file's SHA-256 and size), packs the five parts, and seals them with `openssl cms -encrypt -binary -aes-256-gcm` to the recipient with RSA-OAEP, SHA-256 and MGF1-SHA-256, in DER;
  - uploads the archive as `<yyyy>/<mm>/<yyyymmddThhmmssZ>.tar.p7m` with the Azure CLI image signed in as the backup identity, with `--overwrite false` and `--validate-content`, reads its length and MD5 back, and deletes each archive whose creation is more than 91 days old, leaving any other blob alone;
  - writes one journal line per step and, on any failure, `the backup stopped at the step '<step>'` with whether that night's archive reached the container. Nothing alerts anyone.
- **The helper images.** `infra/compose/compose.backup.yaml` (project `erp-ai-pro-backup`, run only through `docker compose run`) pins Microsoft's distroless debug image of Azure Linux 3.0 and the Azure CLI image by digest, which Dependabot's `docker-compose` ecosystem keeps current. Per volume it defines a reader (read-only mount, the owner's user), a writer (the owner's user) and an owner service (root with `CHOWN` and `FOWNER` alone), all without network; the Azure CLI service runs as 1654, on the default bridge, with a read-only root, its configuration on a tmpfs and the client certificate as its one secret. BusyBox (GPL-2.0) runs unmodified in Microsoft's own image, as a separate program the scripts talk to through pipes; it is linked into nothing of ours and ships in neither edition's images, so the licence policy of [ADR-0030](0030-community-and-enterprise-editions.md), which governs what the product depends on and distributes, is untouched, as it is by the GPL tools of the server itself.
- **The restore.** `scripts/deploy/restore.sh (--latest | --name <archive>) --key <file> [--replace] [--dry-run]` checks that the key is a private key, takes the lock and refuses while a container of the stack runs (a dry run does neither and changes nothing), downloads the chosen archive and checks its length and MD5, opens it, accepts only the five known parts each once and each a regular file, checks every part against the manifest, then per volume gives the top folder the owner and mode its image gives it (only when it differs), empties a volume that holds files only with `--replace`, and fills it as its owner, so a forged archive can create neither a root-owned nor a set-user-id file; it then writes `.env` and `.env.previous` (0600) atomically as the archive holds them, removing `.env.previous` when the archive has none, and shreds the key. `--latest` takes the archive the storage service created last, and a name whose time lies more than an hour from its creation is reported, because whoever holds the backup identity's certificate chooses names.
- **The lock's holders.** `restore.sh` and the backup join `deploy.sh`, `rollback.sh`, the start at boot, the nightly `retain` and the heal service ([ADR-0055](0055-the-production-host-restarts-an-unhealthy-container-and-alerts-nobody.md)); `acquire_release_lock` and `heal.sh` name them.
- **The database's replacement after a restore** is the rename swap: the restored database takes the name `ErpAiPro` and the live one a dated name, so no connection string, setting or secret changes, and `provision.sh` then gives the restored database the template's tier, tags and retention policies again.
- **The rehearsal and its record** are in the [restore runbook](../operations/runbooks/restore.md); the first runs on the production server right after the first deployment.
- **Checks.** `check.sh` checks the backup units (the account, the folder, the script, the conditions, the runtime folder and mask, the India-time timer) and `compose.backup.yaml` service by service, and runs `tests/backup-decisions.sh` (19 scenarios with stand-in commands and the real OpenSSL, tar and coreutils, among them the CMS structure, a held lock, failed reads and uploads, an existing blob, refused certificates and settings, a rebuilt server's restore, a dry run, a wrong key, a manifest mismatch and the archive choice). The bootstrap runner proves the units and the recipient's placement on fresh Ubuntu 22.04, 24.04 and 26.04 runners. `docker-build` and `verify.ts --docker` validate `compose.backup.yaml` with the other two compose configurations.
- **Earlier decisions this changes.**
  - ADR-0050: the database gains long-term retention, and its replacement after a restore is a rename swap instead of re-pointing both connection strings.
  - ADR-0051: `home-address.sh` moves a third rule, the backup account's, in the same run.
  - ADR-0049: a fourth pair of units joins the others, `openssl` joins the bootstrap's packages, and the bootstrap places the recipient certificate.

## Options rejected

- **Azure Backup.** It has no agent for a physical Linux server.
- **Key Vault as the store.** Secrets hold at most 25 KB, the key ring grows by a key every 90 days, and the writer would need a role on a vault that the API or nobody else reads.
- **A container in the attachments account.** It would give that account a second principal and a write-once policy, against ADR-0051's single runtime role.
- **restic, age or GnuPG.** A third-party tool or a key kept outside Key Vault, where OpenSSL, already on the server, seals to a key Key Vault holds.
- **A Key Vault key with `wrapKey` and content encrypted by hand.** `openssl enc` has no authenticated mode, so it means home-made cryptography and a Key Vault role for the server.
- **The runtime's or the migrator's identity for the upload.** The internet-facing API would reach the backups of the proxy's root, and the identity that owns the schema would gain a second purpose.
- **Azure Arc for a managed identity.** A root agent with remote execution on a server ADR-0049 hardened.
- **`docker cp` instead of a helper image.** It needs the stack's containers to exist, which a rebuilt server does not have, and extracts as root.
- **The Azure CLI on the host.** An apt source, a key and an update origin more in the bootstrap, where Microsoft's image needs none.
- **A yearly database copy kept 8 years, immutable long-term backups, versioning or point-in-time restore for the attachments, Azure Backup for blobs and a planned storage failover in the routine rehearsal.** Each costs what the owner chose not to pay: personal data kept longer, a server that can never be deleted, a closed network reopened, or two hours without files.

## Consequences

- **What a loss costs.** A lost or damaged key ring, proxy authority or `.env` comes back as last night's archive held it; without an archive, the key ring's loss signs everyone out once and the proxy's makes every device trust a new root.
- **Nobody is told about a failed backup.** Its journal holds the step it stopped at, and `systemctl --failed` lists the unit; the rehearsal is where a silent failure shows at the latest.
- **The backups cannot be erased early.** Not by the server, its identity or the operator, once the policy is locked; until then an unlocked policy already stops every overwrite and early delete, and only its own removal, which the account's lock also blocks, could undo it.
- **The private key is the backups.** Losing every enabled version of the encryption certificate loses every archive sealed to it, so every version stays enabled until its archives are past 90 days ([secrets runbook](../operations/runbooks/secrets.md), section 5g).
- **Anyone with the backup identity's certificate can add an archive.** It can neither replace nor delete one, and `restore.sh` chooses by the service's creation time and reports a name that disagrees; after a compromise the operator restores an archive from before it, by name.
- **Cost.** The archives are tens of kilobytes each, at ₹3.84 per GB-month and a few operations a night; the long-term database copies cost under ₹1 a month at today's size and at most about ₹65 a month at the database's 2 GB limit; the two certificates add Key Vault's certificate charges when made and at each yearly renewal.
- **Upkeep.** Each Dependabot bump of `compose.backup.yaml` leaves the old helper image on the server until it is removed by hand ([host setup runbook](../operations/runbooks/host-setup.md), "Upkeep"); the encryption certificate joins the yearly rotation of the client certificates.
