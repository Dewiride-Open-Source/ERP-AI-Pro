# ADR-0064: Statutory datasets: TDS under the Income-tax Act, 2025, company tax and depreciation

Status: accepted
Date: 2026-11-08

## Context

- **The roadmap item** `business-building-blocks-statutory-datasets-direct-tax-and-labour` asks for the direct-tax and labour datasets of the statutory parameter store ([ADR-0063](0063-statutory-parameters-dated-rows-in-versioned-sets-typed-reads-by-date-and-the-first-gst-datasets.md)). Root `CLAUDE.md` §5 keeps every statutory rate, threshold, slab, section number and due date an effective-dated parameter record citing its source.
- **Consumers.** `vendors-vendor-tax-profile` maps a vendor to a TDS provision of both Acts; `finance-expenses-tds-computation-engine` computes deductions by the date of credit or payment; `finance-expenses-tds-ledger-challans-and-statements` and `clients-client-tax-profile` read the same sets; the Enterprise `compliance-income-tax-filing-packs-tds` and `compliance-income-tax-filing-packs-company-return` compute advance tax and a company's tax; `procurement-assets-depreciation` depreciates by Schedule II and the income-tax blocks.
- **Owner decisions (2026-11-08).** The labour datasets (provident fund, ESI, professional tax, labour welfare fund, gratuity and bonus) became their own Community item, `business-building-blocks-statutory-datasets-labour`. Every dataset starts with the law in force on 1 April 2026, the first tax year of the Income-tax Act, 2025; the Income-tax Act, 1961 appears only as the crosswalk's old provisions. The sets hold the whole law for Indian companies (every company option, every TDS entry including payments to non-residents with their surcharge, all of Schedule II), foreign companies left out. The Finance Act's one-year rates end with their tax year. The ERP's reading of which Finance Act rate a "rates in force" entry takes, the depreciation allowances with the Notes of the depreciation Table, and TRACES's descriptions of the 1961 provisions in the crosswalk ship now; the TDS section codes of the Rules' forms, the power undertakings' Appendix II, Schedule II's Parts A and B as text and the personal income-tax rates wait for the items that use them. The researchers may read the Gazette copies the web fetch tool saves with the pdftotext that Git for Windows ships.
- **Research (official sources, read 2026-11-08).** India Code, the Income Tax Department's information site, the Ministry of Corporate Affairs, PIB and the Budget site refuse automated fetching; every value was read in the Gazette's own copy (egazette.gov.in), the e-filing portal's copy of a notification, or the Income Tax Department's TRACES pages for their descriptions of the 1961 provisions.
  - *Instruments.* The Income-tax Act, 2025 (30 of 2025, assent 21 August 2025, in force 1 April 2026, section 1(3)) with its Corrigenda of 30 January 2026; the Finance Act, 2026 (4 of 2026, assent 30 March 2026; sections 2 to 129 in force 1 April 2026) with its Corrigenda of 22 April 2026; the Taxation and Other Laws (Amendment) Act, 2026 (21 of 2026, assent 17 August 2026, deemed in force 1 April 2026); the Income-tax Rules, 2026 (G.S.R. 198(E), 20 March 2026, in force 1 April 2026) and their amendments to G.S.R. 961(E) of 30 October 2026, none touching a TDS rate, a threshold or depreciation; the Finance Act, 2025 and the Finance (No. 2) Act, 2024 for the 1961 side of the crosswalk; Schedule II to the Companies Act, 2013.
  - *TDS.* Deduction sits in Chapter XIX-B: section 392 (salary and accumulated balances, six entries) and the three Tables of section 393 (24 entries for payments to residents, 17 to non-residents, 7 to any person), the no-deduction Table of 393(4), the declarations of 393(6), the higher rate of 397(2) (the higher of the entry's rate, the rates in force, or 5 % for purchases of goods and e-commerce and 20 % otherwise; no counterpart of the 1961 Act's section 206AB), rules 162(3)(c) (an inoperative PAN) and 217 (non-residents spared). The rates in force are Part II of the Finance Act, 2026's First Schedule (57 items); section 3(7) adds a surcharge for non-resident payees and 3(16) the cess, which 3(17)(i) spares where the income is paid to a domestic company or another resident. No TDS rate or threshold changed from 1 April to 8 November 2026; the Finance Act, 2026 pointed Note 3 of 393(1) Sl. No. 3 at 3(i), made the supply of manpower "work" (402(47)(f)) and adds depository declarations from 1 April 2027 (393(6)(b)).
  - *Crosswalk.* No official complete mapping exists on a readable site (the department's page answered 503). Each of the 2025 Act's 50 deduction entries has exactly one 1961 source; the Finance Act, 2025 lists the 41 sections under which tax was deducted in 2025-26 and the Finance Act, 2026, section 3(7), lists their successors in parallel; the department's FAQs state six pairs and tie four more through Form 141.
  - *Company tax.* For tax year 2026-27 a domestic company pays 25 % where its turnover in tax year 2024-25 did not exceed ₹400 crore and 30 % otherwise (Finance Act, 2026, First Schedule, Parts I-B and III, Paragraph E); the options of sections 199 (25 %), 200 (22 %) and 201 (15 %) replace sections 115BA to 115BAB; the surcharge is 7 % above ₹1 crore and 12 % above ₹10 crore, and 10 % for the options (25 % for a special purpose vehicle, Taxation and Other Laws (Amendment) Act, 2026, section 6), 25 % on tax under section 195(1)(i) whatever the income (section 3(4)(b), Sl. No. 8) and 12 % on tax charged at the rates of sections 69, 170(5) and 352 (section 3(6)); the Health and Education Cess is 4 %; minimum alternate tax is 14 % (Finance Act, 2026, section 59), its credit clauses omitted and credit brought forward set off only under the options, up to 25 % of the tax; advance tax falls under sections 403 to 410 (15, 45, 75 and 100 % by 15 June, September, December and March) with interest under sections 424 and 425.
  - *Depreciation.* Rule 25 and the Table of Appendix I of the Income-tax Rules, 2026 (138 printed rates by block, nine Notes); section 33 of the 2025 Act (the half rate under 180 days; additional depreciation of 20 %, or 10 % and 10 %), rule 25(2) (the 40 % cap under the options) and 25(7). Part C of Schedule II gives 100 useful lives; the Gazette amendment that set continuous process plant's (G.S.R. 237(E) of 31 March 2014) was not read, and no amendment of Schedule II after G.S.R. 1127(E) of 9 December 2016 was found, though the Companies (Amendment) Acts of 2015 to 2020 were not read.

## Decision

- **Two as-of meanings.** `deductionDate`: the store answers for the credit or payment at which the Income-tax Act, 2025 requires the deduction, which the consumer decides, as the GST engine decides a date of supply. `financialYearStart`: the store answers for the first day of a financial year, and a version of such a set starts only on 1 April (the reader refuses a file otherwise). Company tax, advance tax and income-tax depreciation keep `taxPeriodStart`, the tax period being the tax year. A version's date is the day its instrument came into force or, for a set dated by tax year or financial year, the first day of the first year it governs; a set starts where the ERP's holding starts, 1 April 2026 (owner decision), whatever the date its oldest instrument came into force, and a version may also be the first day after a year's rates end.
- **A `date` cell kind**: canonical `yyyy-MM-dd`, a JSON string in a seed file, read as `DateOnly`, never a key and without bounds; the options' windows (1 March 2016, 1 October 2019, 31 March 2024) are dates an engine checks.
- **Twenty-two sets**, each a seed file and a typed Contracts record:
  - TDS (`Statutory/IncomeTax/Tds/`, `Contracts/IncomeTax/Tds/`), as of `deductionDate`: `income-tax.tds-entries`, `-tds-rates`, `-tds-higher-rates`, `-tds-threshold-limits`, `-tds-no-deduction-cases`, `-tds-declarations`, `-tds-terms`, `-tds-rates-in-force`, `-tds-surcharge-and-cess`, `-tds-rates-in-force-readings` (the ERP's reading) and `-tds-crosswalk` (the ERP's reading);
  - company tax and advance tax (`Statutory/IncomeTax/Company/`, `Contracts/IncomeTax/Company/` and `AdvanceTax/`), as of `taxPeriodStart`: `income-tax.company-rates`, `-company-surcharge`, `-health-and-education-cess` (the cess on the charge, on the tax of section 3(6) and on advance tax), `-minimum-alternate-tax`, `-advance-tax-instalments`, `-advance-tax-interest`;
  - depreciation, as of `taxPeriodStart`: `income-tax.depreciation-rates`, `-depreciation-notes`, `-depreciation-allowances`; and, as of `financialYearStart`, Schedule II's `companies.schedule-ii-useful-lives` (99 lives, continuous process plant left out) and `companies.schedule-ii-limits`.
- **The cess follows its consumer.** The cess on deductions at source and its exception for residents are rows of `income-tax.tds-surcharge-and-cess`, read by the date of deduction with the surcharge and the rates in force; the cess on the charge, on the tax of section 3(6) and on advance tax is `income-tax.health-and-education-cess`, read by tax year.
- **Keys in the Act's own notation**, within the `code` alphabet: an entry `393(1)-6(i)` (the Act writes "section 393(1) [Table: Sl. No. 6(i)]"), a rate case `393(1)-6(i).D(a)` (the Rules write "Sl. No. 6 (i).D(a)"), a crosswalk pair `194C.393(1)-6(i)`; one key column per set.
- **The Finance Act's one-year rows end with their tax year.** The rows of Paragraph E, the company surcharge, both cess sets, Part II, the TDS surcharge and the readings that point at Part II end on 31 March 2027 by a version from 1 April 2027 that quotes what the instruments say: section 3(1) of the Finance Act, 2026 ("for the tax year commencing on the 1st day of April, 2026") for the charge and the advance tax, section 2(90) of the Income-tax Act, 2025 (the rates in force are those of "the Finance Act of the relevant year") for Part II, and the Finance Act's long title ("the financial year 2026-27") for the surcharge and cess on deductions, which rest on it alone. The store then answers no Finance Act rate for tax year 2027-28 until the Finance Act, 2027 is seeded: a version without the Finance Act's rows means that year's Finance Act is not in the ERP, never that nothing is due, and a consumer refuses to compute (owner decision of 2026-11-08). The Act's standing law stays open-ended.
- **The crosswalk is the ERP's reading** (basis `interpretation`): each pair cites the 2025 Act's words, the department's words where it states the pair, and the 1961 provision as the department's TRACES pages describe it, which is not always the Act's heading; pairs whose 1961 side no readable instrument holds (194N's non-filer proviso, 195(6), 197A, 206AA) are absent.
- **The pages.** The list of sets becomes a searched and paged list on the shared kit (ten a page, found by key and by the words of their titles and descriptions), so the page keeps one screen's height whatever the number of sets; a set whose entries hold more than six columns, the source included, shows them as cards at every width, because no table shows that many columns of sentences legibly. A card puts each heading above its value on a narrow box and beside it, at most two fifths of the width, on a wider one, so a long heading never leaves its value without room.
- **Left out and named**: Schedule II Part C IV(i)(b) until G.S.R. 237(E) is read; the TDS section codes 1001 to 1067 of Forms 138, 140 and 144 (1007, 1010, 1025 and 1036 printed in no instrument read); Appendix II; Schedule II's Parts A and B as text; the personal income-tax rates (Part III, Paragraph A of the Finance Act, 2026 and section 202(1)); foreign companies' own tax; the Income-tax Rules amendment G.S.R. 241(E) of 31 March 2026, unread; and the item of Part II that section 3(8) of the Finance Act, 2026 applies to a benefit or perquisite or a virtual digital asset paid for in kind (section 393(1) [Table: Sl. No. 8(iv). Note 2, Sl. No. 8(iv). Note 6 and Sl. No. 8(vi). Note 6]), though `income-tax.tds-surcharge-and-cess` holds the section itself.

## Options rejected

- **A slash in codes** (`393(1)/6(i)`): the hyphen form needs no change to the reader and matches how the Rules cite a cell.
- **Composite keys**: each row has one natural key in the Act's notation; the labour datasets, keyed by State and slab, bring them if they need them.
- **A neutral `inForce` as-of for Schedule II**: Schedule II governs financial statements by financial year, which `financialYearStart` says and the 1 April rule protects.
- **Schedule II from 2014-04-01**: the store would claim the financial years 2014-15 to 2025-26 on readings the research did not complete; the owner's holding starts on 1 April 2026.
- **One cess set for every consumer**: a TDS computation would read one Finance Act value by the tax year and the rest by the date of deduction.
- **Keeping last year's Finance Act rates until the next are seeded**: the store would state rates no instrument gives for that year.
- **Seeding from the department's FAQs and rate charts**: they are not the instruments and contradict them in places (194N's non-filer rule, the senior citizens' limit).
- **The Act's year in a key** (`tds.ita2025.…`): every set starts under the current Act, and the versions carry the years.
- **A card per set on the list page**: thirty cards outgrow a full-page capture on a phone, and the labour datasets add more.

## Consequences

- A new Finance Act each year is a release before 1 April: a version from 1 April of its tax year in every set whose rows ended, citing it.
- An `amount` can exceed a 32-bit integer (₹400 crore); no consumer reads one into `int`.
- The TDS engine chooses which event decides the deduction date, which Part II item a "rates in force" entry takes where the reading does not say (393(2) Sl. Nos. 7, 8, 9 and 17 depend on the kind of income), and the surcharge band; the salary entries of section 392 other than sub-section (7) and section 393(1) [Table: Sl. No. 8(iii)] take the rates of Part III (Finance Act, 2026, section 3(10)), not Part II, so they have no reading, and their rates are the item `business-building-blocks-statutory-datasets-personal-income-tax`.
- The fixed-assets item adds the ERP's reading that links an asset kind to a Schedule II entry and an income-tax block, and the rows left out above when it needs them.
- The GST due-date rules, ten columns with their source, now show as cards too.
- **Earlier decisions this changes.** ADR-0063: its as-of dates gain `deductionDate` and `financialYearStart`, its cell kinds gain `date`, and its list of sets as cards becomes a searched list.
