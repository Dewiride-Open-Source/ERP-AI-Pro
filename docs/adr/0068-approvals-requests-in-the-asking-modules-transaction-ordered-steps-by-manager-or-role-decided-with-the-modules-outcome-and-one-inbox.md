# ADR-0068: Approvals: requests in the asking module's transaction, ordered steps by manager or role, decided with the module's outcome, and one inbox

Status: accepted
Date: 2026-11-27

## Context

- **The roadmap item** `business-building-blocks-shared-minimal-approvals-building-block` asks for an approval request bound to any record by a generic reference, the states submitted, approved, rejected, returned and withdrawn, approvers resolved by manager or role, time limits, endpoints and an inbox page. It is the permanent Community approval mechanism (ADR-0030): Community modules call it and never the Enterprise workflow engine, which later plugs in behind the approval-routing contract of `business-building-blocks-edition-extension-points`.
- **Its consumers** are later items: a vendor's KYC, a bill before it is posted, a payout batch (the checker is not the maker), a reimbursement (the manager, then Finance), a timesheet (the manager, with a reason to reject), a leave request and a purchase request.
- **One context per unit of work.** A module writes through its own context, and writes across modules go through integration events (ADR-0060), which deliver later, as the system actor, with no way to tell the decider that the record refused the outcome. Two in-process paths already exist where a building block and a module must commit together: the admission of a person at sign-in, which a building block asks of a module through a port (`IPersonAdmission`, ADR-0039), and the numbering's tables lent to an issuing module's context (ADR-0066).
- **Managers on a date.** A person's manager on a date is their latest position starting on or before it, and no reporting cycle exists on any date (ADR-0042); a manager deactivated by the nightly account check keeps their team until an administrator records a new one (ADR-0047).
- **Owner decisions (2026-11-27).** While the ERP has one user nobody else could approve, so administrators switch approval off per kind of request and change its working days; any administrator decides a waiting request in place of its approver, never their own; time limits count working days Monday to Friday, ending at midnight India time, public holidays counting until a holiday calendar exists; lateness is shown in the ERP and the daily reminder comes with the email item; delegation comes with the first consumer that needs it; each person sees the requests they asked for, those waiting for them and those they decided, administrators every request; finished requests are kept for ever; one person may decide two steps of a request; the texts and the six permission sentences as proposed.

## Decision

- **A building block and a Platform module, as numbering is split.** `BuildingBlocks.Approvals` owns the schema `platform_approvals` (`ApprovalsDbContext`: `ApprovalRequests`, `ApprovalSteps` and `ApprovalKindSettings`), the catalogue of the kinds modules declare, the routing, the working days, the submission, the decisions, the inbox and the kinds' settings; `Platform/Approvals` (schema `null`, flag `Erp.Modules.Platform.Approvals`) is the HTTP surface and implements the block's port `IReportingManagers` over the new `Identity.Users.Contracts` query `IReportingLines`.
- **A kind is declared by the module that asks for it.** `services.AddApprovalKind<TContext, THandler>(new ApprovalKind(key, title, description, subjectType, steps, workingDaysToDecide))`: a key of three lower-case kebab segments under the module's prefix, the change key of the record it approves as the generic reference, one to five ordered steps decided by the asker's manager or by a role, and the working days of each step.
- **A request is submitted in the asking module's transaction.** The module's context maps `ApprovalRequests` and `ApprovalSteps` (lent, left out of its migrations, as the numbering's tables); `IApprovals.SubmitAsync` adds the request and its planned steps last in the command that changes the record, so both commit together or neither; one waiting request per kind and record (a filtered unique index); `WithdrawAsync` closes it first in a module command that cancels the record, and `StatusOfAsync` answers the record's latest request. While a kind is switched off, `SubmitAsync` answers approved without a request and the module applies the approval itself.
- **A decision runs on the kind's module context with the module's outcome.** `Platform/Approvals` takes every decision through one route; the block runs it in a unit of work on the kind's own context, under the application lock `platform_approvals.<request id>` and the version the decider saw, and when the request reaches a final state it calls the module's `IApprovalOutcomeHandler` inside that transaction. So the record moves with the decision or nothing changes, the person who decided is the actor of both change records under one correlation id, and a refused outcome is the decider's answer. A handler may refuse an approval; it accepts a return, a rejection and a withdrawal; the command it runs checks none of the decider's permissions. This is the third sanctioned in-process path between a building block and a module, beside `IPersonAdmission` and the lent numbering tables.
- **Who decides.** Steps are planned at submission: a manager step goes to the asker's manager on the day of submission, walking up past a manager who is deactivated, has no account or submitted the request, and to the administrators when nobody is left; a role step goes to the holders of a built-in role. Nobody decides a request they asked for or submitted (the Community maker-checker); an administrator decides any other waiting request in place of its approver, recorded as such; one person may decide two steps; sending back or rejecting needs a reason. Every final state is final, and asking again after changes is a new request.
- **Time.** A step is due at the end of its working days after it starts, Monday to Friday, at midnight India time; lateness is read from the due time and shown, never stored, and no job runs.
- **Who sees what.** Six permissions, held by Employee (`platform.approvals.requests.read`, `.decide`, `.withdraw`) and administrators alone (`.read-all`, `.decide-any`, `platform.approvals.kinds.change`); the page `/platform/approvals` lists what waits for the caller, what they asked for, what they decided, the kinds of request with their steps, and for administrators every request.
- **Records.** Requests, steps and kind settings are recorded under `platform.approvals.requests`, `platform.approvals.steps` (filed under the request) and `platform.approvals.kind-settings`; nothing is deleted. No integration event is published.
- **The routing stays in one internal class**, so the approval-routing contract of `business-building-blocks-edition-extension-points` replaces where a request goes without changing what a module calls.

## Options rejected

- **Approvals in the block's own context, decisions announced by integration events**: the record would move later than its decision and as the system actor, a record that refuses the outcome would leave an approved request over an unapproved document, and the block has no outbox to publish from.
- **Each consuming module hosting its own decision routes**: the "never your own request" rule and the decision dialog would be repeated in every module, and one that forgot the rule would break the maker-checker.
- **Approvers resolved when the decision is taken**: the asker would not see the route, and the inbox could not be one query.
- **A job that moves a request whose approver left**: an administrator deciding in place serves the same need without moving requests behind people's backs.
- **Steps naming permissions instead of roles**: every consumer would declare a permission per step.
- **The person who asked approving their own request when nobody else can**: switching a kind off says honestly that nobody approved it.

## Consequences

- **A consumer writes little**: two `DbSet`s, one `AddApprovalKind`, one `SubmitAsync` in the command that changes its record, and one outcome handler that runs its own command; its next migration takes the lent tables in without an operation.
- **An outcome handler runs inside the decision's transaction and lock**, so it must be quick and call no outside service, as an integration event subscriber.
- **With the module's flag off** consumers still submit, and nobody can decide until it is on again; switching a kind off also removes its maker-checker.
- **Public holidays count as working days** until `hr-core-leave-management` brings State holiday calendars, which the working days then read.
- **Delegation, named approvers and reminders** come with their items: `finance-reimbursements-approval-routing` and `timesheets-approvals-and-reminders` add delegation and the engagement-lead rule, and `business-building-blocks-minimal-transactional-email-sender` the daily reminder job over the steps' due times.
