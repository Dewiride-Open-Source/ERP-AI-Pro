# ADR-0043: Change records and the append-only audit schema

Status: accepted
Date: 2026-10-16

## Context

- **What existed.**
  - The audit stamps of ADR-0015 keep who created a row and who last changed or deleted it, and when; nothing kept what a change replaced.
  - `BuildingBlocks/Auditing` owned the schema `audit` with the table `SecurityEvents` (ADR-0038): rows inserted through `ISecurityEventRecorder`, never updated by the code, and deleted once a day by the API's own `SecurityEventRetentionSweeper` past `Erp:Platform:Auditing:SecurityEventRetention`. Nothing in the database stopped the API's login from updating or deleting a row (residual risk 2 of `docs/security/auth-threat-model.md`).
  - A position is never edited, only recorded and withdrawn, and a department move or a designation rename holds for every date (ADR-0042), so a trail of changes is the only history of those changes.
- **The roadmap scope** asked for an EF Core `SaveChanges` interceptor recording append-only entries (the actor, the time, a before and after diff, the client address and the correlation id) into the schema `audit` for changes to people, roles and departments, a filterable and exportable audit endpoint, a timeline component, and an architecture test requiring aggregates to implement `IAuditable`.
- **The law, as read on 2026-10-16** (the Gazette of India on egazette.gov.in, meity.gov.in and cert-in.org.in):
  - Companies (Accounts) Rules, 2014, proviso to rule 3(1), inserted by G.S.R. 205(E) of 24 March 2021 and applying, after G.S.R. 247(E) of 1 April 2021 and G.S.R. 235(E) of 31 March 2022, to financial years beginning on or after 1 April 2023: accounting software that keeps the books of account records "audit trail of each and every transaction, creating an edit log of each change made in books of account along with the date when such changes were made and ensuring that the audit trail cannot be disabled". It names the books of account, not the records of people or of the organisation. Verified.
  - Companies (Audit and Auditors) Rules, 2014, rule 11(g), inserted by G.S.R. 206(E) of 24 March 2021 and amended by G.S.R. 248(E) of 1 April 2021: the auditor reports whether the audit trail "has been operated throughout the year for all transactions recorded in the software", "has not been tampered with" and "has been preserved by the company as per the statutory requirements for record retention". Verified.
  - Companies Act, 2013, section 128(5), for the books of account: eight financial years by secondary sources; official text not yet read, because mca.gov.in and indiacode.nic.in answered 403 to every automated read, the last on 2026-10-16.
  - Digital Personal Data Protection Rules, 2025 (G.S.R. 846(E) of 13 November 2025), rules 6(1)(e) and 8(3): logs and the personal data of each processing are kept for at least one year, after which rule 8(3) has them erased "unless further retention is required for compliance with any other law"; both come into force eighteen months after publication (rule 1(4)), about 13 May 2027. Verified.
  - Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023, section 8(7): personal data is erased once it is reasonable to assume that its purpose is no longer served, unless retention is necessary for compliance with a law. Verified.
  - CERT-In Directions of 28 April 2022, direction (iv): the logs of every ICT system are kept for a rolling 180 days within Indian jurisdiction (ADR-0038). Verified.
  - No verified rule sets a period for records of people, departments or access rights other than those two minimums.
- **EF Core 10 and SQL Server** (the EF Core `release/10.0` source and Microsoft Learn):
  - `SaveChangesAsync` calls every interceptor's `SavingChangesAsync` before it detects changes again and saves, so an entity an interceptor adds there is saved by the same call; interceptors run in the order they were added.
  - Without an explicit transaction, a save of two or more commands runs in a transaction EF Core begins and commits itself (`BatchExecutor`); the execution strategy retries the state manager's save without running the interceptors again; a `DbUpdateConcurrencyException` reaches `ThrowingConcurrencyException` and never `SaveChangesFailed`.
  - With `CascadeDeleteTiming.OnSaveChanges`, cascades are applied after the interceptors ran, so no interceptor sees a cascade-deleted dependent.
  - A table excluded from migrations yields no migration operation (`MigrationsModelDiffer`), so one entity type can be mapped in every context while one context creates its table.
  - `DENY` takes precedence over every grant except for members of `sysadmin`, the `dbo` user and the owner of the securable, and a member of `db_owner` can revoke it. `TRUNCATE TABLE` needs `ALTER` on the table. `CREATE ROLE` needs the database permission `CREATE ROLE`, and a role created without `AUTHORIZATION` is owned by the user that created it. `DENY` on a schema needs `CONTROL` on it.
  - The `json` type accepts an object or an array only, never a scalar or `null`, and may round a JSON number.
- **Owner decisions of 2026-10-16**, each the recommended option:
  1. Change records are kept eight financial years after the financial year of each change: the owner's choice, one period that also suits the books of account the ERP keeps later, not a period a law sets for people records. Security events stay at one year.
  2. The database stops the running ERP from changing or deleting its history: the API's database user only inserts into `audit`, and only the migrator deletes what is past its retention, when it runs.
  3. The old and new values of a person's details are kept in full, readable only by those who may read the history. PAN, Aadhaar, bank details and salary are never readable there.
  4. Administrators alone read and download the history, on a new page "Audit trail".

## Decision

- **What is recorded.** Every entity type a catalogue context maps whose CLR type implements `IAuditable` and that is not owned is recorded (`RecordedEntityTypes`, `BuildingBlocks.Persistence/Auditing/Changes`): no switch, setting or per-type opt-out exists, because the books rule says the trail "cannot be disabled". Today that is the `identity_users` tables `Users`, `Departments`, `Designations` and `Positions`, `files.Attachments`, and the sample types of the persistence tests. The module flag `Erp.Modules.Platform.Audit` gates the routes that read the history and nothing else. A type whose rows are inserted once and never changed implements the marker `IAppendOnly` (`BuildingBlocks.Kernel/Domain`) instead, its row being its own record, deleted only by retention: `ChangeRecord`, `SecurityEvent` and `ApiStartup`, which stays an `AggregateRoot`.
- **Change keys.** A recorded type declares a stable key in its entity configuration, `builder.RecordChangesAs("identity.users.people")`: three lower-case kebab-case segments `<domain>.<module>.<feature>`, unique, whose first two segments are the module's permission prefix for a type of a module context. The keys are `identity.users.people`, `identity.users.departments`, `identity.users.designations`, `identity.users.positions` and `platform.attachments.files`. The history and the web app name a record's type by its key, never by its table, so a table can be renamed without touching the history; renaming a key needs a migration that rewrites the stored rows and an ADR.
- **The subject.** A record is filed under a subject, by default the changed entity itself. `builder.FileChangesUnder(p => p.UserId)` files a position's changes under its person, so a person's history shows their moves: the subject's key is that of the type the foreign key references, and its id the foreign key's value. The annotation names a required single-property foreign key to a recorded type.
- **The table.** `audit.ChangeRecords`, created by the migration `AddChangeRecords` of `AuditingDbContext`:

  | Column | Type | Holds |
  |---|---|---|
  | `Id` | `uniqueidentifier`, primary key | `ChangeRecordId`, a version 7 GUID set in code |
  | `OccurredAt` | `datetimeoffset` | the UTC time of the change, exactly the stamp it gave the row (`CreatedAt`, `ModifiedAt` or `DeletedAt`) |
  | `Operation` | `tinyint` | `ChangeOperation`: `Created` 1, `Updated` 2, `Deleted` 3, `Restored` 4; values never reused |
  | `EntityType`, `EntityId` | `varchar(64)`, `uniqueidentifier` | the changed record's key and id |
  | `SubjectType`, `SubjectId` | `varchar(64)`, `uniqueidentifier` | the subject's key and id |
  | `ActorId` | `uniqueidentifier` | `IActorContext.ActorId`: the person's Entra object id, `ActorIds.System` or `ActorIds.Anonymous` |
  | `ClientAddress`, `CorrelationId` | `varchar(64)`, null | the origin of the request |
  | `Changes` | `json`, null | the value entries; SQL `NULL` when there are none, because `json` refuses a JSON `null` |
  | `ValueCount` | `smallint` | the number of value entries |

  Indexes: `OccurredAt`; (`EntityType`, `OccurredAt`); (`EntityId`, `OccurredAt`); (`SubjectId`, `OccurredAt`); (`ActorId`, `OccurredAt`); `CorrelationId` filtered to rows that carry one. Filters and sorts read these columns only, never the JSON. No column is generated by the database (no identity, default, computed column or row version), so EF Core inserts the records with a plain `INSERT`, never `MERGE` or `OUTPUT`, which a user that may only insert can run (`AppendOnlyAuditTests`).
- **The values.** `Changes` is an array of entries `{ property, kind, before, after, references }` in the model's property order, written by `ChangeValueWriter` with `Utf8JsonWriter`. `property` is the camelCase path, a member of a complex type dotted (`price.amount`). `kind` comes from the model: `text`, `number`, `boolean`, `date`, `instant`, `enum`, `list`, `reference`, `id` or `binary`, so a reader shows a value by its kind and never guesses from its text. A string or a boolean is itself; a number is its invariant text in a string, so `json` never rounds it; an enum is its camelCase member name, the wire convention; a GUID or strongly-typed id is its `D` text through its value converter; `Currency` is its code; `DateOnly` is `yyyy-MM-dd`; `DateTimeOffset` is UTC in the round-trip `O` format; bytes are base64; a primitive collection (`User.Roles`) is an array of such values. `references` is the key of the recorded type a single-property foreign key points at.
- **What a record leaves out.** The key, which has its own column; the `IAuditable` and `ISoftDeletable` members, because the operation already says a delete or a restore; `IVersioned.RowVersion`, whose new value does not exist before the save; discriminator, computed and store-generated properties; and a property its configuration marks `ExcludeFromChangeRecords()`: today `User.LastSignedInAt`, because every sign-in is a `SignedIn` security event already.
- **The capture.** `AuditingSaveChangesInterceptor(IActorContext, IRequestOrigin, TimeProvider)` (`BuildingBlocks.Persistence/Auditing`), which every catalogue context already runs, stamps and then records in `SavingChangesAsync`, from one read of the clock:

  | Entry once stamped | Record | `Changes` |
  |---|---|---|
  | `Added` | `Created` | every recorded property's value, `after` only |
  | `Modified`, `IsDeleted` false → true | `Deleted` | the other recorded properties the same save changed |
  | `Modified`, `IsDeleted` true → false | `Restored` | the same |
  | any other `Modified` | `Updated` | the recorded properties marked modified whose original and current values differ by their value comparer; when none is left, as for a sign-in that moved only `LastSignedInAt` and the stamps, no record |
  | `Deleted`, a hard delete such as a withdrawn position | `Deleted` | every recorded property's original value, `before` only |

  It first detaches the records an earlier save of the same context left tracked (`Forget`, which also runs from `SavedChangesAsync`, `SaveChangesFailedAsync`, `SaveChangesCanceledAsync` and `ThrowingConcurrencyExceptionAsync`, because a concurrency failure skips `SaveChangesFailed`), and throws `InvalidOperationException` for a `Modified` or `Deleted` entry of an `IAppendOnly` type, for an owned navigation or a complex collection on a recorded type, and for a context that holds recorded entries and maps no `ChangeRecord`. `ChangeRecord.Record` refuses a value longer than its column, as `SecurityEvent.Record` does.
- **Atomicity.** The records join the same `SaveChangesAsync`, so they commit and roll back with the change:
  - inside `EfUnitOfWork`, which every module command runs in (the sign-in admission's `AdmitPersonCommand` included), in its transaction; a transient failure replays the whole use case after `ChangeTracker.Clear()`, which drops the earlier records, so one set is written, and a later domain-event round that throws rolls the records of the earlier rounds back;
  - without an explicit transaction (the attachments upload and `AttachmentService.DeleteAsync`), in the transaction EF Core begins for the change and its records; a retried save resends the same tracked records.

  A change whose record cannot be written is not saved, so a sign-in whose admission writes a record fails as one whose `SignedIn` event cannot be written does (ADR-0038). `ChangeRecordingTests` and `ChangeRecordAtomicityTests` prove it against SQL Server, a retried save among them.
- **The origin.** `IRequestOrigin` (`ClientAddress`, `CorrelationId`; `BuildingBlocks.Application/Origins`) is `SystemRequestOrigin` in every host: no address, and the current activity's trace id when there is one. `AddErpEndpoints` replaces it with `HttpRequestOrigin` (`BuildingBlocks.Endpoints/Origins`): the client address forwarded headers resolved (an IPv4-mapped address as IPv4) and the correlation id of `ICorrelationIdFeature`, else the trace identifier when it fits its column, both from `RequestOrigins`, which `SignInAudit` uses too, and the system origin when there is no `HttpContext` (a hosted service of the API).
- **One table, every context.** `ModuleDbContext.OnModelCreating` maps `ChangeRecord` after the context's own configurations (`ChangeRecordMapping`), so a context saves its records with its own changes; every context but `AuditingDbContext` maps the table with `ExcludeFromMigrations()`, so only `AddChangeRecords` creates it. The type lives in `BuildingBlocks.Persistence`, which every context references and which `BuildingBlocks.Auditing` references in turn. `PersistenceTests` exempts exactly `ChangeRecord` from the rule that a catalogue context maps only its own assembly's types, and `ChangeRecordTests` checks that every context maps it, excluded from migrations unless its schema is `audit`. A module's model snapshot takes the excluded mapping in at its next migration, which adds no operation for it.
- **Append-only in the code.** The capture refuses a change or a deletion of an `IAppendOnly` entry. `BulkWriteGuardInterceptor` also refuses `ExecuteUpdate` on an `IAppendOnly` type and `ExecuteDelete` on an `IAuditable` type, which would bypass the capture (a position); `ExecuteDelete` of `ChangeRecord` and `SecurityEvent` stays open to retention. The architecture tests (`LayerTests`) refuse calls to `Update`, `UpdateRange`, `Attach` and `AttachRange` of `DbContext` and `DbSet` in product assemblies, because an attached entity's original values are its current ones, and in module assemblies the `State` setter of `EntityEntry` and `ChangeTracker.TrackGraph` for the same reason, calls to `ExecuteSql*`, `SqlQuery*`, `FromSql*` and `GetDbConnection`, and any use of `Microsoft.Data.SqlClient` or `System.Data.Common`, because SQL a module sends itself writes no record; a building block may, as `TransactionLocks` does.
- **Append-only in the database.** `AddChangeRecords` creates the role `erp_audit_append_only` (`AuditingDbContext.AppendOnlyRole`) when it does not exist and denies it `UPDATE`, `DELETE` and `ALTER` on the schema `audit`, `ALTER` because `TRUNCATE TABLE` needs it; the statements run through `EXEC(N'…')` so the idempotent script compiles. Its `Down` revokes the denial, removes the role's members and drops it. The API's database user is a member: in production the contained user of `first-deployment-production-database-provisioning`, which also keeps it out of `db_owner`, `db_securityadmin` and `db_ddladmin`, away from `ALTER ANY ROLE` and from the ownership of `audit`; in the local container stack the SQL login `erp_local`. The denial binds every member except `sysadmin`, `dbo` and the schema's owner; a `db_owner` member is bound but could revoke it. `AppendOnlyAuditTests` prove it with a user `WITHOUT LOGIN` in `db_datareader`, `db_datawriter` and the role, under `EXECUTE AS USER`: it inserts into both tables, saves a recorded sample and a security event, and is refused every `UPDATE`, `DELETE` and `TRUNCATE TABLE` of either table. Host processes and tests run as the developer's own sysadmin login and CI's container stack as `sa`, so nothing is enforced there.
- **The migrator's rights.** `AddChangeRecords` needs the database permission `CREATE ROLE` and `CONTROL` on the schema `audit`, which a migrator user holds without a grant only when it owns the schema. On the owner's database, whose schemas `node scripts/ef/ef.ts update --all` created as `dbo`, the local container migrator login `erp_local_migrator` is granted both ([local development](../guides/local-development.md), section "Containers"). No migrator holds `db_owner`.
- **Retention in the migrator.** `IRetentionSweep` (`BuildingBlocks.Persistence/Retention`, registered with `services.AddRetentionSweep<TSweep>()`) deletes what is past a retention period, and `RetentionRunner` runs every sweep, each in its own scope, logging a failed one at `Error` and going on, because a deletion that can wait never blocks a deployment. The migrator runs it after the seeders on `migrate` and alone on the command `retain`, which applies no migration and so can run every day without moving the schema ahead of the running API; `status` and the test databases run no sweep. `AuditRetentionSweep` (`BuildingBlocks.Auditing/Retention`, registered by `AddErpAuditing`) deletes the security events older than `Erp:Platform:Auditing:SecurityEventRetention` (one year) and, while `Erp:Platform:Auditing:ChangeRecordRetentionYears` is set (1 to 30), the change records made before 1 April, in IST, of the financial year that many years before the current one: with 8, the financial year 2035-36 deletes those made before 1 April 2027, the records of 2026-27 and earlier. It deletes in `DELETE TOP` statements of 1,000 rows (`AuditRetentionSweep.BatchSize`) until one deletes fewer, because one statement holding 5,000 locks on an index escalates to a table lock that would hold back the API's inserts. `retain` exits 1 when a sweep failed, after running the others, so a daily run notices it; `migrate` logs the failure and still exits 0. Only the migrator registers `RetentionRunner`, so the API host cannot resolve it (`RetentionRegistrationTests`). Without the setting every change record is kept, and the figure 8 lives in `infra/appconfig/defaults.json`, never in code. `AuditRetentionSweepTests` prove both sides of the financial-year boundary (31 March 23:59:59 and 1 April 00:00 IST). `SecurityEventRetentionSweeper` and `Erp:Platform:Auditing:SweepInterval` are gone, and the API deletes nothing in `audit`.
- **Personal data in the history** (owner decision 3). The values keep their own copies of names, work emails and phone numbers, readable only by holders of `platform.audit.changes.read`; ADR-0038's rule that a row names no one binds the security events, not the values of the change records. Actors are never copied: the history shows each actor by the name their record holds when it is read. PAN, Aadhaar, bank account numbers and salary components are never readable in the history: `hr-core-employee-master-extension` adds a way to record that such a field changed without its values before the first of them exists. Erasing the values of people who have left belongs to `operations-hardening-data-privacy-compliance-dpdp`, under the migrator's principal, because the API cannot.
- **Reading the history.** `IChangeHistory` (`BuildingBlocks.Auditing/Changes`; scoped `ChangeHistory` over `AuditingDbContext`, read without tracking):
  - `ListAsync(ListRequest)`: newest first (`occurredAt:desc`, the id breaking ties), sortable by `occurredAt` only, filtered by `entityType`, `entityId`, `subjectType`, `subjectId`, `actorId`, `operation` and `correlationId` (`eq`, `ne`, `in`) and by `occurredAt` (those and the comparisons).
  - `ExportAsync(FilterRequest)`: the matching records made up to the moment the export starts, oldest first, read in keyset pages of 500 on `OccurredAt` (a page that ends inside a run of equal times takes the rest of the run), each page buffered under the retrying execution strategy, which buffers every result itself; an export of more than `ChangeExport.MaxLines`, 100,000 CSV lines counted as the sum of `max(1, ValueCount)`, is refused with 422 `audit.export-too-large` (`ChangeHistoryErrors`).
  - Names, once per page (`ChangeNames`): subjects and referenced records through the registered `IRecordNames`, each naming the keys it lists, actors through `IPersonNames` (`BuildingBlocks.Application/Actors`). `UserRecordNames` of `Identity/Users` implements both over records deleted or not (a person's display name, and for an object id that two records name, the record not deleted before the newer one; a department's or a designation's name), and `AttachmentRecordNames` of `BuildingBlocks.Attachments` names files by their file name. A name that cannot be found stays empty. An actor is a person, the ERP itself (`ActorIds.System`) or someone not signed in (`ActorIds.Anonymous`), `ChangeActorKind`.
- **The routes.** The module `Platform/Audit` is the history's HTTP surface, with no schema of its own, like `Platform/Attachments`: route group `/api/platform/audit`, flag `Erp.Modules.Platform.Audit` (enabled by default and seeded enabled under both labels), permissions `platform.audit.changes.read` and `platform.audit.changes.export` (`AuditPermissions`), declared for no role besides Administrator (owner decision 4).
  - `GET /api/platform/audit/changes` (`Platform.Audit.ListChanges`, `read`) answers a page of change records with their names.
  - `GET /api/platform/audit/changes/export?filter=` (`Platform.Audit.ExportChanges`, `export`; request record `ExportChangesRequest`) streams `text/csv; charset=utf-8` with `TypedResults.Stream` under `Content-Disposition: attachment; filename=change-history-<yyyyMMdd-HHmmss in IST>.csv`, under a request timeout of five minutes and the rate-limiter policy `platform.audit.exports` (a `ConcurrencyLimiter` of two exports for the process, no queue; 429 `rate-limit.exceeded` beyond it). It is the command `ExportChangesCommand`, because it records each export as the security event `ChangeHistoryExported` (`SecurityEventKind` 8) with the number of records (`Detail` `records:<count>`); an export whose event cannot be written is refused with 500, and a refused export records nothing. A request whose `Sec-Fetch-Site` is present and not exactly `same-origin` (`FetchMetadata.IsFromThisOrigin` of `BuildingBlocks.Endpoints/Security`, the rule of the antiforgery reissue and the anonymous sign-out): another site's top-level navigation, which the SameSite=Lax session cookie still reaches, any `same-site` request, or an address typed into the browser (`none`), is refused with 400 `request.invalid` before the command runs (another site's image or frame carries no session cookie and is answered 401 `request.unauthenticated` before the route), so no other site can make an administrator's browser take the history out under their name; the page's own fetch says `same-origin` and the web server's calls send no such header. Every refusal is answered before the first byte; a failure after it can only end the connection.
  - The file follows RFC 4180 (commas, CRLF, every cell in double quotes, UTF-8 with a byte order mark for Excel), with one line per changed value and one for a record without values, and a cell that begins with `=`, `+`, `-`, `@`, a tab, a carriage return, a line feed or a full-width `＝`, `＋`, `－` or `＠` gets a leading `'` (OWASP, CSV injection); [HTTP conventions](../architecture/http-conventions.md), section "Files on the wire", lists the columns.
- **The page.** `/platform/audit`, "Audit trail" (`features/platform/audit`; its navigation entry for holders of `platform.audit.changes.read`): the changes as a timeline grouped by day in IST, each with its time, who did what to which record, the changed values shown by their kind with names where known, and links to that record's history and to that actor's changes; filters by kind of record, kind of change, dates, actor and record; pages; and, for holders of `export`, a CSV download with the list's filters, fetched by the browser (`downloadFromApi`, `shared/api/download/download.ts`) and disabled with a message while the list holds more than 100,000 records. `Timeline` (`packages/ui/src/components/timeline`) is a generic composite shown on the kitchen sink, and `ListFeed` (`shared/lists/feed`) frames a list shown as a feed as `ListTable` frames a table.
- **Architecture rules.** Besides the rules above:
  - every entity type a module context maps implements `IAuditable` or `IAppendOnly`, and every aggregate root implements `IAuditable` unless it is `IAppendOnly`;
  - a recorded type declares a well-formed key, unique and, in a module context, under its module's prefix; has one key property whose provider value is a `Guid` known before the save; is changed by no delete of a principal (every foreign key `Restrict`, `NoAction` or `ClientNoAction`); has no owned navigation and no complex collection; and files its changes only under a required foreign key to a recorded type;
  - every registered `IRecordNames` names recorded keys only, each once;
  - only `BuildingBlocks.Persistence` and `BuildingBlocks.Auditing` reference `ChangeRecord`, so no module reads `audit` through its own context (`LayerTests`).
- **Earlier decisions this changes.**
  - ADR-0015: the auditing interceptor also records each change, takes the request's origin and refuses a change to an append-only row; the bulk guard also refuses `ExecuteDelete` on an auditable type and `ExecuteUpdate` on an `IAppendOnly` one.
  - ADR-0016: the unit of work commits the change records with the use case.
  - ADR-0019: a second named rate-limiter policy, `platform.audit.exports`.
  - ADR-0021: the migrator runs the retention sweeps after the seeders and has the command `retain`, and its user needs `CREATE ROLE` and `CONTROL` on `audit`.
  - ADR-0022: attachments are recorded and named by their file name; a download changes nothing, so `files.DownloadRedemptions` stays the record of who opened which file.
  - ADR-0038: the API's daily `SecurityEventRetentionSweeper` and `SweepInterval` are removed, so a security event past its year is deleted when the migrator next runs rather than within a day, which also changes the accepted criterion of `authentication-login-hardening` that a daily sweep deletes them (owner decision of 2026-10-16); the trail is append-only by database rights; an export of the history is the security event `ChangeHistoryExported`.
  - ADR-0039: a person's admission writes change records of their own record, the person being the actor of their first one.
  - ADR-0041: the two permissions of the history, Administrator's alone.
  - ADR-0042: positions are filed under their person.
  - The roadmap scope: the interceptor that records belongs to `BuildingBlocks.Persistence`, because `BuildingBlocks.Auditing` references persistence and not the reverse, and the architecture rule covers every entity type of a module context, not only aggregate roots.

## Options rejected

- **A second context sharing the connection and transaction, or raw SQL after the save.** Neither commits atomically with a save that runs without an explicit transaction, and raw SQL after the save is not retried.
- **An opt-in marker apart from `IAuditable`.** A type could be forgotten, and the trail "cannot be disabled".
- **The actor's name stored with each record.** It copies personal data into every row and goes stale; the name is read when the history is.
- **Ledger tables.** Their rows can never be deleted, so no retention period is possible, and their digests on SQL Server need a shared access signature, which the repository never uses.
- **System-versioned temporal tables.** No actor for a hard delete, no client address or correlation id, and one history table per table.
- **Triggers.** They know no actor and complicate every migration; `finance-core-journals-and-posting` decides whether the books need changes made directly in the database captured.
- **Ownership-chained procedures in `audit`** through which the API deletes past a floor the database keeps, with the daily sweep kept in the API (owner decision 2).
- **`db_owner` for the migrator.** Least privilege: `CREATE ROLE` and `CONTROL` on `audit` are enough.
- **`ApiStartup` made auditable.** A migration renaming its columns, and a record per start that duplicates its own row.

## Consequences

- Every recorded change costs one more row in the batch of the change, and a change whose record cannot be written is not saved.
- Rows past their retention leave only when the migrator runs: at every deployment (`migrate`) and every day once `first-deployment-host-bootstrap-runbook-and-scripts` schedules `retain`. Between runs a security event can outlive its year.
- `sysadmin`, `dbo`, the owner of `audit`, the members of `db_owner` and the migrator's user can still rewrite the trail, and a compromised API can still insert forged rows or change business tables with raw SQL that writes no record (residual risk 2 of the threat model).
- Nothing is enforced on developer machines or in tests, which run as sysadmin; the role binds the local container stack's api login and production's api user only.
- The history keeps names, work emails and phone numbers for eight financial years. Rule 8(3) of the Digital Personal Data Protection Rules, read literally, has the logs of a processing erased after their year unless another law requires more, so keeping the history of people records longer rests on its being part of the employment record; `operations-hardening-data-privacy-compliance-dpdp` reviews that and erases the values of people who have left.
- The eight years rest on the owner's choice; `finance-core-journals-and-posting` confirms section 128(5) from its official text and decides whether changes made directly in the database must be captured for the books.
- A recorded type uses no owned type, complex collection, key the database generates or foreign key that a principal's delete cascades or sets to null, product code calls neither `Update` nor `Attach`, and module code neither sets an entry's state nor reaches the database beside EF Core.
- Module code could still hide a change from the capture by setting a recorded property's original value (`PropertyEntry.OriginalValue`, `OriginalValues.SetValues`).
